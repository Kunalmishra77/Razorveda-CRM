# Your day in the CRM

For the tele-sales team. Two screens, and everything you need is on them.

---

## Signing in

Your email and your password. No app, no code — that is only for admins.

**One device at a time.** Signing in somewhere else ends your other session. If
you are suddenly signed out, somebody has signed in as you, or an admin has locked
the account. Ask.

---

## Your worklist

The first screen you see. It is **ordered by what to do next**, not by date — work
down it.

| Band | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Overdue** | You promised a call on a day that has passed. |
| **Due today** | You promised today. |
| **Repeat due** | She is running out and will reorder. Highest conversion of anything on this list. |
| **New today** | Assigned to you today, never called. |
| **Ageing** | Running out of validity. After that it stops being yours. |

You cannot re-sort it, and that is deliberate. A list sortable by value gets
worked from the top down by value, and the small leads sit untouched for a full
validity window — which is exactly what happened before.

### The 72-hour rule

**A lead you have not called within 72 hours of it being assigned goes back to the
pool.** At 48 hours your admin is told.

It is not a punishment. A lead sitting untouched on your list is a customer
waiting for a call nobody is making — better that somebody else gets it.

"Called" means **logged**. If you rang her and did not record it, the system
cannot tell the difference.

---

## Making a call

Click the lead. You see her number, her history, and what happened last time.

The number is yours to dial from your own handset. **Every reveal and every copy
is recorded** — not to catch you out, but because full phone numbers are visible
to you by design, and a record is what makes that safe for everybody.

> **One thing to know.** Copying four numbers within ninety seconds locks your
> account automatically. That pace looks like a script rather than a person. If it
> happens innocently — a stuck key, or copying a batch into your phone before a
> call block — tell your admin what happened. They can unlock it, and they are
> asked to look at your history before they speak to you.

### After the call

Log it. **The disposition is required** — the system will not accept a call
without one, because a call with no outcome is a call nobody can learn from.

- **Connected?** Say honestly whether she picked up. Your connect rate is not a
  score you are ranked on; it tells your admin whether the numbers on that channel
  are any good.
- **Follow-up date.** Some dispositions require one. If you said you would call
  Tuesday, put Tuesday in — it is what puts her at the top of your Tuesday list.
- **The remark** is stored exactly as you type it. Hinglish is fine. Nothing is
  rewritten.

## Booking an order

On the lead, **Book an order**.

Add the products, set quantities and prices, and tick **upsell** for anything you
added that was not in her original cart.

### Two payment boxes, never one

**Prepaid ₹** and **COD ₹**. They must add up to the order value — the button
stays disabled until they do.

This matters more than it looks. How much a customer pays up front is the best
predictor there is of whether the parcel comes back, and that could never be
measured before because it was typed into one box as free text.

### Your credit, while you sell

As you build the order you see three numbers:

```
Order ₹3,000  ·  Company base ₹500  ·  Your credit ₹2,500
```

**Company base** is what Razorveda had already committed before you touched the
order — what was in the cart she built herself. **Your credit** is the rest: what
you added.

You never type the company base. It cannot be got wrong, forgotten, or argued
about, and that is the point.

If it says **your credit is pending**, the product's base price has not been
confirmed by an admin yet. **Book the order anyway** — the sale is real and gets
recorded. Your credit follows when they confirm the price. Tell them, so it does
not sit.

### Booked is not paid

Your credit **realises when the parcel is delivered**, not when you book it. If it
comes back, the credit is taken off again.

So a delivered ₹2,000 order is worth more to you than a booked ₹5,000 one that
returns. Prepaid orders come back far less often. Getting even a part payment up
front is worth the conversation.

## Repeat customers

The highest-value calls you will make all week.

When a customer's order is delivered, the system works out when she runs out and
puts her back on **your** list five days before — she is yours because you sold to
her. She already knows the product and already trusts you.

She appears in the **Repeat due** band. She is not a new lead.

---

## What you cannot do, and why

Not distrust. Each one exists for a reason worth knowing.

- **You cannot export anything.** There is no button. Nothing to be talked into.
- **You cannot see another rep's leads or customers.** Not hidden — the database
  genuinely will not return them.
- **You cannot mark your own order delivered.** Delivery is what pays you, so it
  comes from the courier, not from the person being paid. You *can* confirm an
  order with the customer, and cancel one if she changes her mind.

---

## When something looks wrong

- **A lead vanished from your list** — 72 hours with no logged contact, or an
  admin reassigned it. Your admin can see exactly what happened and when.
- **Your credit looks wrong on an order** — check whether it says *pending*. If
  not, ask your admin: the split is on the order and cannot be edited by hand,
  which is what makes it worth checking rather than arguing about.
- **You are signed out unexpectedly** — somebody signed in as you, or the account
  is locked. Ask, and say what you were doing.
- **An error message you do not understand** — tell your admin the exact words.
  Every message in this system is meant to say what happened and what to do next.
  If one does not, that is a fault worth fixing.
