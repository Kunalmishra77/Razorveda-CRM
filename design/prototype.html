01 — Architecture

How data moves through the system

Nine upload channels feed one ingestion pipeline. The pipeline produces one customer record and one order ledger. Everything downstream — assignment, scoring, incentive, every report — reads from those two things and nothing else.

End-to-end flow source → ingestion → core → intelligence → screen

The seven-stage pipeline, live click to run a simulated batch

STAGE 1
Fingerprint
File hashed. Duplicate uploads blocked before anything is written.
STAGE 2
Column map
Saved template matched on header signature. No AI call today.
STAGE 3
Normalise
Phone, date, payment split, encoding, disposition, SKU.
STAGE 4
Identity
Matched against 4,812 customers. New vs existing decided here.
STAGE 5
Validate
Row rules plus column-shift detection on the whole batch.
STAGE 6
Review
Admin sees only what failed. Clean rows are never shown.
STAGE 7
Commit
Leads land in the unassigned pool, ready for the admin to distribute.

If you later want the strongest control optional, Phase 6

Click-to-call with masking

A cloud dialler (Exotel, Servetel) connects both legs. The rep taps Call and never sees the number at all. Costs roughly ₹0.60–0.90 per minute plus a monthly rental, and calls get recorded automatically — which also gives you call-quality audit. This is the only control that actually removes the risk.

Partial masking

Show 98••••6231 by default with a one-tap reveal that is logged and rate-limited. Reps can still dial from their handset but cannot skim a page of numbers at a glance. A middle path if a dialler is too much right now.

Desktop shell

A Windows app that blocks screen capture at the OS level. Real protection against screenshots, but only on company machines and only on Windows. Worth it if reps work from an office floor, pointless if they work from home on their own laptops.

Assignment rules admin decides, system enforces the guardrails

RULE 1 · Leads wait in a pool
Nothing is assigned automatically. Committed leads land in the unassigned pool and stay there until an admin distributes them.
RULE 2 · Filter, tick, assign
Filter by source, state, product interest or age. Tick a block, pick a rep, assign. Sixty leads move in three clicks with one log entry — not sixty rows of copy-paste.
RULE 3 · Existing customers are flagged
If a lead already belongs to a rep, the system says so before you hand it elsewhere. You can override; the override is logged.
RULE 4 · Overload is warned, not blocked
Assigning 60 more leads to a rep sitting on 120 open ones throws a warning. Your call, but you make it with the number in front of you.
RULE 5 · Untouched leads come back
Assigned and not touched in 48 hours triggers an alert. At 72 hours the lead returns to the pool automatically, so a block never rots in one rep's queue the way 174 leads did in August.
RULE 6 · Suggested split is advisory
The system shows what a balanced distribution would look like based on open workload and last month's yield. Accept it, edit it, or ignore it entirely — it never assigns on its own.
RULE 7 · Every move is logged
Who assigned what, to whom, when, and why. Transfers and recalls too. There is no way to move a lead silently.

Performance score measures reps · does not assign leads

This score exists for reports and incentive, not for allocation. Since you assign by hand, its job is to tell you who is actually performing and why — and to feed the assignment quality audit that makes your own distribution decisions sharper each week.

Delivery quality at 20% is the deliberate change. In a COD business a rep booking ₹3L at 41% RTO destroys more margin than one booking ₹1.5L at 3%. The current scoreboard cannot see the difference — it treats a booked order and a delivered one as the same event.
Bayesian shrinkage, k = 30 leads. adjusted = (n × observed + 30 × team mean) ÷ (n + 30). A rep with twelve leads and one lucky order cannot top the table — which is exactly what the current Scoreboard allows.
Source-mix neutralisation. Scores are computed within source cohorts and re-weighted, so a rep you loaded with delivered-data repeats is not compared naively against one you gave cold Meta leads. Without this, the score would mostly be measuring your assignment choices rather than their work.
